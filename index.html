About Me

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Algorithm Engineering Group at the Hasso Plattner Institute (HPI) in Potsdam, Germany. I work in algorithmics and computational complexity, with a focus on parameterized algorithms, temporal graphs, and scheduling.

Before joining HPI, I was a postdoctoral researcher at Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, first in the Department of Industrial Engineering and Management with Danny Hermelin and Dvir Shabtay, and then in the Department of Computer Science with Meirav Zehavi and Klim Efremenko. I received my doctoral degree from TU Berlin in 2020, supervised by Rolf Niedermeier.

Research Interests

  • Temporal Graphs
  • Parameterized Complexity
  • Graph Algorithms
  • Scheduling
  • Algorithmic Aspects of Tournaments

Much of my work studies temporal graphs — graphs whose edges are only present at certain points in time — and how classic problems such as paths, separators, matchings, and connectivity change in this setting. I am also interested in parameterized algorithms and kernelization for graph problems, scheduling, and computational questions about knockout tournaments.

Funding

DFG Research Grant since 2025 · Principal investigator
Advancing Algorithmic Temporal Graph Theory (project no. 565381415)

Developing new algorithmic tools for temporal graphs through temporal graph classes and structural temporal parameters, and applying them to NP-hard problems from transportation and social networks via parameterized algorithms.
